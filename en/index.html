平安京 Heian-kyo

Walk the capital of the year 900 in your browser

Heian-kyo is the city that became today's Kyoto. This is a 3D miniature world in your browser that reconstructs the capital about a century after it was laid out, built on the national elevation data of Japan. You play the tin robot "the Iron Child": look down on the city from the sky, walk its streets and listen to the people who live there. There is no winning or losing and no fixed story. Go wherever you like.

Heian-kyo in the year 900

Emperor Kanmu moved the capital to Heian-kyo in 794. This reconstruction shows the city about a hundred years later, in the year 900 (the third year of the Shotai era), under Emperor Daigo. Fujiwara no Tokihira is Minister of the Left and Sugawara no Michizane is Minister of the Right. In the first month of the following year, Michizane is demoted to a post in distant Dazaifu and leaves the capital. It is a time when Rajomon Gate, the Great Hall of State, the palace and the Toji and Saiji temples all stood together.

The grid of streets was never filled with houses, and parts of the western half were already turning into farmland. The reconstruction keeps those open lots and marshes as they were.

The buildings in the year 900

  • The Great Hall of State (Daigokuden) burned in 876 and was rebuilt in 879. The hall you see is the second one.
  • Otenmon Gate burned in the Otenmon Incident of 866 and was rebuilt. Thirty-five years later, people who saw the fire still live in the city.
  • Rajomon Gate fell in a great storm in 816. The gate you see is the second one.
  • The five-storey pagoda of Toji Temple took decades to build and was finally completed at the end of the ninth century.
  • The imperial palace (Dairi) keeps its form from the founding of the capital.

What you can do

Fly over the city

The drone tour flies through 19 places, from Rajomon Gate along Suzaku Avenue to the palace and Toji Temple. Drag and scroll to look from just above the ground to straight overhead. The grid, about 5.2 km north to south and 4.5 km east to west, sits in its real landscape of mountains and rivers.

Walk the streets

Start from any of 50 places and click the ground (tap on a phone) to walk. You can also go inside some of the buildings. The townspeople's chatter appears in speech bubbles as you pass.

Visit the landmarks

Talk with the townspeople

The game has about 3,000 townspeople, and what they say changes with the place, the hour, the season and the weather. Rank, household and whether they side with the Minister of the Left or the Minister of the Right shape how they talk. Earlier events such as the Otenmon Incident and the demon of the Pine Grove of the Banquet come up as rumours. What you hear is kept in your notebook, and each day is written up in a diary. Every line of dialogue is available in English.

A calendar that moves

Days pass from the 25th day of the second month of the year 900 to the year 922, following the lunar calendar of the time, including long and short months and leap months. Once you pass the Shotai Incident on the 25th day of the first month of 901, Michizane's ox cart no longer appears, and the townspeople change what they say and where they go. The nobles' ox carts leave their mansions for the palace on schedule. The seasons turn: plum, cherry, new leaves, autumn colour and bare branches, with sunrise and sunset moving through the year. Each day is clear, cloudy or rainy. The East and West Markets open in alternate halves of the month. At night only bonfires and oil lamps break the darkness, as they did then.

Take photos and share them

Photo mode saves the view as an image. A caption with the date, weather and place is added, and the share button posts it straight to social media. You can hide the robot to capture just the streets, or shoot in a black-and-white gekiga (dramatic manga) style with ink lines and screentone. Your photos can also serve as backgrounds for your own manga, illustrations and videos (see the questions below for the conditions).

The night sky of the time

The stars are placed where they stood around the year 901, worked back from a modern star catalogue by calculating the precession of the Earth's axis. Polaris, today's pole star, was about 7° from the celestial pole then, so it does not stay fixed in the north. The moon waxes and wanes, and the planets follow the calendar. You can show the lines and names of the Chinese constellations used at the time, and on clear nights townspeople tell stories about the stars.

Requests

Find a lost child, deliver a love letter, catch a thief of dried fish, look into the pillars of Rajomon Gate, and more. These quest-like requests deepen your ties with the townspeople, bring new stories, and open the way into the palace and the mansions of the nobility.

The research behind it

Buildings, streets and daily life were reconstructed by comparing city plans, recorded street dimensions, excavation reports and court diaries. The research notes separate what the sources confirm from what is estimated. All 66 notes are published, in Japanese, as a companion to the reconstruction.

What is HakoniwaKit?

HakoniwaKit is a project to recreate towns, castles and regions, historical or imagined, as miniature worlds (hakoniwa) you can walk in your browser. Heian-kyo is the first. More are planned.

Questions

Why the year 900?
The planned buildings all stood together at this time, and in the first month of the next year the Shotai Incident drove the Minister of the Right, Sugawara no Michizane, out of the capital. As the calendar moves on you reach that incident, the change of era to Engi (901) and the death of Fujiwara no Tokihira (909).
Is it free?
Yes. No sign-up is needed. It runs when you open it in a browser.
Does it work on a phone?
Yes. Drag to look around and tap the ground to move. The first download is large, so Wi-Fi is recommended.
How accurate is it?
The terrain uses the elevation model of the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan, and the streets are laid out from dimensions recorded in historical texts. Where direct evidence is scarce, such as building details and commoners' houses, the reconstruction is an estimate, and the research notes (Japanese) state how certain each part is.
Can I use my photos in my own work?
You can use them as backgrounds in personal works such as manga, illustrations and videos. When you publish, please credit "HakoniwaKit Heian-kyo". For commercial use, please get in touch.
Is my progress saved?
The date and what you have heard are kept in your browser, and the next visit continues from there.
